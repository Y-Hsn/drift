Drift · artist guide

Making a Drift sketch

Drift is a museum for music, and a sketch is one of its rooms. Drift brings the songs, the sound and every button. You draw the room, from a blank page: ink on paper, a darkroom, a tape deck, anything you can picture.

What a sketch is

Drift comes with three rooms, Sunset, Vinyl and Paper Theatre. Every other room is a sketch: a folder with four kinds of file.

sketch.jsonIts name, who made it, one line about it, and the words of the menu along the top.
sketch.jsYour drawing: a short JavaScript file that paints the Now page from the song that is playing.
PicturesPaper, a wall, brushes, a photo: PNG, JPG, WEBP or BMP files you draw with.
A fontOptional: one .ttf or .otf for titles, if its licence lets you share it.

Zipped, the folder is a .driftsketch file that anyone with Drift can import.

It is safe to share. sketch.js runs in a sealed box. It cannot read or write files, reach the internet, or start anything on the computer. It can only draw, and ask Drift to play, pause, skip, seek or open the lyrics. If it takes too long, Drift stops it and shows a message.

Your materials

Every time Drift needs a picture, it hands your draw function the song. A good room is built from these, so that every song makes its own picture:

Its colourssong.colors: a palette taken from the cover. Use it and the room changes with every song.
Its shapesong.shape: the loudness of the whole song in 160 steps. Hills, grooves, a brush stroke, a skyline.
Its coverDraw it as it is, crop it, mask it, tint it, use it as a stamp or a reflection.
Time and wordssong.progress, the seconds, and song.line, the line being sung right now.

Designing one that fits the music

  1. Let the song lead. Hundreds of different covers will pass through your room. Build with song.colors and song.shape rather than fixed colours, so the room answers the music. Fix one or two things (paper, ink) and let each song bring the rest.
  2. Start from one idea. "Every song is a brush stroke on rice paper." "Every song is a photo developing in a tray." Pick each choice to serve that sentence and leave out the rest.
  3. Make the song's progress visible. People want to see where they are. Let something move or fill as the song plays, and let a click on it move through the song.
  4. Quiet behind, clear in front. The title and the sung line must read on every cover. Keep what sits behind words low in contrast.
  5. Keep it dark enough to rest in. Music plays at night and during games. Large bright areas tire the eyes; save light for what matters.
  6. Less motion is more. With fps = 0 your room redraws only when something changes, which is lightest on the computer. Use motion where it means something, slowly.

Test it with five covers

Play songs whose covers look like these, plus one song with no cover, and try the calm view (double-click). If the room works for all of them, it is done.

Making it, step by step

  1. In Drift, open Settings, then Sketches, then New sketch… and give it a name.
  2. Drift makes the folder, switches to the new room and opens the folder and this guide. The room starts as a blank page: a dark ground, the cover, the song's shape as one line, the title. It is there so you can see something work straight away. Replace all of it.
  3. Open sketch.js in any text editor (Notepad works; VS Code colours the code).
  4. Change something and save. Drift redraws within a second, every time. Keep Drift beside your editor.
  5. Put your pictures and font in the same folder and use them by file name.
  6. If something is wrong, Drift shows the problem and the line in sketch.js on the Now page. Fix it and save.
  7. Fill in by and about in sketch.json, then press Export… in Settings to make the .driftsketch file.

To use a sketch someone sent you: Settings → Sketches → Import…. Ctrl + T walks through every room.

A first room

This whole sketch.js paints each song as one ink stroke across paper. The stroke is the song's shape; the part already played is wet ink, the rest is a pencil line; a click on it moves through the song.

var fps = 0;

function draw(g, song, t) {
  var W = g.width, H = g.height;
  g.fill('#efe9dc').rect(0, 0, W, H);                   // the paper
  g.texture('paper.png', 0.5);                          // a picture in the folder, tiled

  var y = H * 0.55, x0 = 60, x1 = W - 60;
  var line = [];
  for (var i = 0; i < song.shape.length; i++) {
    var x = x0 + (x1 - x0) * i / 159;
    line.push(x, y - song.shape[i] * 120 + drift.noise(i / 8) * 10);
  }
  var cut = Math.max(2, Math.round(song.progress * 159) * 2 + 2);
  g.noFill();
  g.stroke('rgba(40,30,20,0.25)', 1).curve(line);       // pencil: the whole song
  g.stroke(song.colors.dark, 6).curve(line.slice(0, cut)); // ink: what has played
  g.noStroke();

  g.fill('#2a2118').text(song.title, x0, H * 0.72, { size: 34, font: 'title' });
  g.fill('#6b5d4d').text(song.line, x0, H * 0.72 + 52, { size: 14, font: 'title', italic: true });
}

function press(x, y) { drift.seek((x - 60) / (g.width - 120)); }
function drag(x, y)  { drift.seek((x - 60) / (g.width - 120)); }

Keeping it light

  • Keep fps = 0 unless the room really moves. Drift then draws about four times a second while a song plays.
  • Put what does not change every frame (paper, a wall, the whole song's shape) in g.layer.
  • One picture can have up to 30 000 drawing steps, and each call may take a quarter of a second. Beyond that, Drift stops the call; after three stops it pauses the sketch and says so.

Files

Pictures: PNG, JPG, WEBP or BMP, 8 MB each. Fonts: .ttf or .otf. A whole sketch: 24 MB and 24 files at most, all in one folder (no sub-folders). File names use letters, numbers, spaces, dots and dashes. When importing, Drift checks that every picture and font really is one and leaves anything else out.

Before you share it

  • It works with the five test covers, a song with no cover, nothing playing, and the calm view.
  • The title and the sung line read clearly, and words stay clear of the menu and the player.
  • The song's progress can be seen, and the room is comfortable after an hour at night.
  • You made the pictures or may share them; the font's licence allows sharing.
  • name, by and about are filled in.
Drift keeps this guide in its Sketches folder, next to your sketches.
